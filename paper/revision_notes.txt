MAIN FINDINGS
The unit is a model/dataset/horizon setting after averaging three training seeds.
Ratios across settings are geometric means; wins are descriptive, not significance.
RG-TTA versus fixed TTA:
  Real streams: MSE ratio 0.949946, wins 61/96, total-time ratio 1.412118.
  Synthetic streams: MSE ratio 0.918919, wins 85/128, total-time ratio 1.442155.
  Overall: MSE ratio 0.932090, wins 146/224, total-time ratio 1.429204.
Do not claim the former speedup: corrected RG takes longer on this matrix.
The DLinear aggregate worsens (ratio 1.022469); retain that negative result.
RG-EWC improves its own baseline but has little aggregate accuracy advantage over
RG-TTA and much greater measured time. RG-Dyna improves its own base but does not
beat ordinary TTA in the aggregate. Baseline choice changes the interpretation.

RESPONSE TO AE CONCERN 1: SCALE AND NONCONVEX ADAPTATION
Completed evidence: Table III, 24 settings, ETTh1/H96, three seeds, two backbones,
four configurations. Larger models have 3.30–3.51 million parameters. Reported
update scopes are head-only, adapter-plus-head, and full-model. iTransformer
adapter-plus-head is best among its RG configurations. PatchTST full updates are
best among its RG configurations, while its larger head/adapter RG versions are
worse than matched TTA. This supports feasibility and backbone-dependent behavior,
not universal superiority or foundation-model scaling.
Changes: removed blanket convexity/convergence claims; stated that generic exact-GD
results do not prove properties of Adam, moving objectives or retrieval. Reported
actual parameter counts and complete-job peak CUDA memory.
Still open: multiple datasets/horizons at larger scale; adaptation-only memory;
shared-source checkpoint hashes for the main/focused policy comparisons. The
6.4M released-code models are baseline-only evidence, not RG scaling evidence.

RESPONSE TO AE CONCERN 2: MULTIVARIATE REGIMES
Completed evidence: Table IV, 27 settings on ETTh1, Weather and CovShift. The
baseline implementation already includes per-covariate means/std in addition to
target 5D statistics. Added signature comparisons include contemporary correlation,
lag-1/6/24 autocorrelation and lag-one cross-channel correlation. The new signature
also changes block scaling. No consistent forecast improvement was observed.
Changes: removed any claim that dependence features are a validated novel method.
Explained the target-heavy similarity weights and the scope of the synthetic test.
Still open: detector-level shift discrimination and shifts that make covariates
necessary for prediction. CovShift preserves target AR dynamics and tests changed
association, not a comprehensive conditional-shift taxonomy.

RESPONSE TO AE CONCERN 3: DYNA-TTA AND RETRAIN FAIRNESS
Completed evidence: six released-code comparisons using unchanged DynaTTA adapter
source at 03c3cd9568ee9affa5617efa3c8dbc0224bf9a06 with restored TAFAS framework at
139bf980671da4daad728a0fc21d8df508b9203d. ETTh1/ETTh2, H96, seeds 0/1/2. All three
policies share a source checkpoint within each seed. Scores use all 3,389 standardized
multivariate test windows per run. Main-table local reimplementation is explicitly
called Dyna-style. These protocols are never pooled or used for a direct RG-versus-
released-DynaTTA claim. Exact paper-table reproduction is not established: complete
paper-specific configs/checkpoints were unavailable, and the PDF endpoint remained
behind an access challenge. The evaluated released implementation does not impose
500-window truncation; this is not evidence that the AE's paper protocol is wrong.
The EMA/warmup study has 18 settings. EMA .7 beats the default .1 on both streams;
on Weather it reverses the ranking against RG. No equal-budget validation search
was performed, and test-selected settings must not be relabeled as fair tuning.
Retrain has a different recipe: 20 initial epochs, up to 30 per update, expanding
history, separate preprocessing/training path. Runtime includes a partial-model
branch even when the full model is selected. Do not market its runtime as an
optimized always-retrain baseline or an intrinsic RG speedup.
Still open: independently audit upstream label timing/prediction adjustment and run
RG inside that same protocol; matched validation tuning; optimized retraining;
simple frozen/naive/closed-form baselines across the full matrix.

NOVELTY AND CLAIM LIMITS
The defensible contribution is the controller combination and a transparent
empirical evaluation of where it helps or hurts. Adapters, dynamic gating, or the
new feature vector cannot be presented as independent established novelty. The
current ablations do not isolate retrieval from early stopping and learning-rate
changes strongly enough to claim retrieval alone explains the gains. A narrower
empirical paper is supported; a strong state-of-the-art or theory paper is not.
Do not add untested mechanisms to the manuscript as completed contributions.
